Resolve: HR Workflow and Judgment

Follow a fictional HR request from policy lookup to a draft response and simulated review. Inspect the boundary between routine support and a concern that needs specialist judgment.

Challenge

A correctly identified HR topic can still hide a concern requiring specialist handling. The design question is where automation should stop and a person should take ownership.

Approach

Defined the workflow and evaluation questions, then built the prototype with AI-assisted development. The browser demo uses rules and fixed-template responses. Review is simulated, activity history is temporary, and no employment action is executed. An optional model screen is a separate experiment.

Outcome

The original rules passed 60 development cases and failed every complete pass condition in 16 developer-authored paraphrases. A narrow correction now passes 1 of those 16 known cases. Four specialist-routing misses remain. I preserved the original report and kept the production hold; no independent validation or business outcome is claimed.

Work Experiment Lab

Prioritize workplace improvements, compare a fictional three-arm experiment, and decide what changes when speed improves but accuracy fails. Record a simulated decision with an owner and next review.

PrioritizationExperiment designAdaptationDecision evidence
Try the synthetic experiment lab ↗︎
Read case study +

Problem

A high score or a faster answer does not settle which initiative deserves investment. Leaders need to inspect dependencies, accuracy safeguards, capacity, and contradictory evidence.

My contribution

Defined the fictional workplace problem, prioritization and decision rules, experiment structure, and portfolio narrative. Built the interactive prototype with AI-assisted development in Lovable.

Demonstrated behavior

The faster, less accurate scenario reduces illustrative AI-arm accuracy to 78%, below a 90% demonstration safeguard, and disables scaling. Browser checks verified a simulated revision decision, persistence, and reset. These are software behaviors on invented aggregates, not findings from a participant study.

Limits

Northstar and every result are fictional. No real AI calls or employment decisions occur. History is editable local browser state. Print/PDF output remains unverified; no causal effect, statistical significance, or enterprise readiness is claimed.

Project scope: reference tools, proposed pilots, and synthetic demonstrations. No measured business outcomes or production readiness are claimed. Resolve simulates review and remains unsuitable for real HR use. Its evaluation evidence and limits are available with the source.
